How to reduce a photo to 20 KB, 50 KB or 100 KB for online forms

Updated October 19, 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer: Crop the photo to just your face or signature, set the exact pixel size the form asks for, then compress to just under the KB limit. With CompressZone's Compress to KB tool you type the limit and it finds the best quality that fits automatically.

Why forms have strict size limits

Application portals for exams, jobs, scholarships and visas often handle millions of uploads, so they cap each photo at a small size — commonly 20 KB for signatures, and 20–100 KB for photos. A typical phone photo is 2–5 MB, which is 20 to 250 times too big.

The trick is to remove what the form doesn't need: extra background, extra pixels and hidden metadata. Then the remaining image can keep good quality inside the limit.

Step by step: how to reduce a photo to a specific KB size

1

Crop to the subject

Open the photo in Crop Image and crop tightly around your face (or signature). Less area means more quality per kilobyte.

2

Set the required pixel size

If the form says something like 200 × 230 px or 600 × 600 px, open Resize to exact pixels and enter that size with Crop to fill.

3

Compress to the KB limit

In Compress to KB, type the maximum size (for example 50 KB). The tool tries different quality levels and keeps the best one that fits. A green tick confirms it's under the limit.

4

Check before uploading

Use the before/after slider to check your face or signature is clear, then download the JPG.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my photo get rejected even though it's under the limit?

Many forms also check pixel dimensions and file type. Make sure the image is a JPG with the exact width and height requested, not just under the KB limit.

How do I compress a signature to under 20 KB?

Sign on plain white paper, photograph it in good light, crop tightly, then compress to 20 KB. Signatures compress very well because they're mostly white.

Can I do this on my phone?

Yes. All the tools work in mobile browsers — choose the photo from your gallery and download the result.

With CompressZone, yes: your photo is processed in your browser and never uploaded to any server.
