    How PNG compression works

    PNG is a lossless image format, so it can't simply be saved at a "lower quality" like JPG. Instead, CompressZone uses color quantization: it reduces the image to an optimized palette of up to 256 colors and then re-compresses the data. For screenshots, logos, icons and illustrations, this typically cuts file size by 50–80% with little or no visible change.

    Transparent backgrounds are kept. For photographs saved as PNG, converting to WebP or JPG usually gives much smaller files.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does compressing a PNG keep the transparent background?

    Yes. CompressZone keeps the alpha channel, so transparent and semi-transparent areas stay transparent.

    Is PNG compression lossless?

    Standard PNG encoding is lossless. CompressZone's default mode reduces the number of colors, which is slightly lossy but usually invisible. Set quality to 95% or higher for fully lossless output.

    Why is my compressed PNG still large?

    Photos with many colors and gradients don't compress well as PNG. Convert them to WebP or JPG instead — they are often 70–90% smaller.

    PNG or WebP: which is better for a website?

    WebP lossless files are about 26% smaller than PNG on average and also support transparency, so WebP is usually better for websites. Keep PNG when you need maximum compatibility with design tools.
