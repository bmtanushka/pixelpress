How to remove location data from photos before you share them

Updated October 19, 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer: Check the photo with an EXIF viewer. If it contains GPS coordinates, remove them with Remove EXIF data before sharing — or turn off location in your phone's share options or camera settings.

What your photos can reveal

Phones store EXIF metadata inside every photo: the device model, date and time, camera settings and — when location services are on — the precise GPS coordinates where the photo was taken. If you share the original file by email, messaging apps, cloud links or marketplaces, anyone who receives it can read that location, which may be your home or workplace.

Many large social networks strip location from public posts, but you can't rely on every app or website doing it.

On iPhone

When sharing from the Photos app, tap Options at the top of the share sheet and turn off Location. To stop saving location in new photos, go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Camera and choose Never.

On Android

Open your camera app's settings and turn off location tags (the option name varies by phone maker). In Google Photos, check the privacy settings for an option to remove location from items you share.

Step by step: how to remove GPS location from a photo

1

Check the photo

Drop the photo into the EXIF viewer. If it shows "Contains GPS location", the coordinates are listed with a map link.

2

Remove the metadata

Open Remove EXIF data and add the photos. Location, camera details and other metadata are removed automatically, without changing image quality.

3

Share the clean copy

Download the cleaned photos (or a ZIP) and share those instead of the originals.

Frequently asked questions

Does WhatsApp remove location from photos?

Messaging apps often re-compress photos, which may remove metadata, but sending a photo as a document or file can keep it. Removing it yourself first is the safest option.

Does removing EXIF data reduce quality?

Not with CompressZone for JPG, PNG and WebP — the metadata is removed without re-compressing the image.

Will the photo still have the right orientation?

Yes. The orientation flag is kept so the photo doesn't appear sideways.
