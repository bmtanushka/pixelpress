JPG vs PNG vs WebP vs AVIF: which image format should you use?

Updated October 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: use WebP for almost all images on websites. Use JPG for photos that must open everywhere (email, print, older software), PNG for graphics that need lossless quality or editing, and AVIF when you want the smallest possible files and your audience uses up-to-date browsers.

JPG (JPEG)

JPG has been the standard format for photographs since the early 1990s. It uses lossy compression, discarding detail the eye barely notices, so photos can be 10 times smaller than an uncompressed image. Every device, app and website supports it. JPG has no transparency, and repeated saving slowly degrades quality.

Use it for: photos for email, printing, documents and any place where compatibility matters most. Compress JPEG files.

PNG

PNG is a lossless format: every pixel is stored exactly. That makes it ideal for screenshots, logos, icons, diagrams and images with text, and it supports full transparency. The downside is size — a photo saved as PNG is often 5–10 times larger than the same photo as JPG.

Use it for: graphics with sharp edges, transparent images, and files you'll keep editing. Compress PNG files with smart color reduction.

WebP

WebP was developed by Google for the web. It supports lossy and lossless compression, transparency and animation in one format. Google reports that lossy WebP images are 25–34% smaller than comparable JPGs, and lossless WebP images are about 26% smaller than PNGs. Every modern browser supports WebP, including Safari since version 14 (2020). In our own JPG vs WebP test, WebP files were 24% smaller than JPG at the same quality setting.

Use it for: nearly every image on a website. Convert JPG to WebP or PNG to WebP.

AVIF

AVIF is based on the AV1 video codec and usually achieves the smallest files of the four, often around half the size of a comparable JPG. It supports transparency, HDR and wide color. Encoding is slower, and some older browsers and many desktop apps still can't open it.

Use it for: performance-critical websites, ideally with a WebP or JPG fallback using the HTML <picture> element.

Simple rules for choosing

  1. On a website? Use WebP (or AVIF with a WebP fallback).
  2. A photo for email, print or an older app? Use JPG at 80–90% quality.
  3. A logo, icon, screenshot or image with text? Use PNG, or WebP at 90%+ quality on the web.
  4. Need transparency? Use PNG or WebP — never JPG.
  5. Always resize first. Scaling a 4000 px photo down to 1920 px saves far more than any format change. Resize images.

Frequently asked questions

Is WebP better than JPG?

For websites, yes. WebP files are typically 25–34% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality and also support transparency and animation. JPG remains better for maximum compatibility with older software and devices.

Is AVIF better than WebP?

AVIF usually produces smaller files than WebP at the same quality, but it encodes more slowly and has slightly less support in older browsers and desktop software. WebP is the safer default; AVIF is a good upgrade for performance-focused sites.

Which image format is best for SEO?

Search engines don't rank formats directly, but faster pages rank and convert better. Modern formats like WebP and AVIF reduce page weight and improve Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), a Core Web Vitals metric. Also use descriptive file names and alt text.

Does converting between formats reduce quality?

Converting to a lossy format (JPG, lossy WebP or AVIF) re-encodes the image and can lose some detail. At 80% quality or higher, the difference is usually invisible. Always convert from the highest-quality original you have.
