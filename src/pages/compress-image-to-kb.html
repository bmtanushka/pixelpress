How compressing to a target size works

Many job portals, exam and government forms only accept photos under a fixed size, such as 20 KB, 50 KB or 100 KB. Instead of guessing a quality setting, CompressZone searches for you: it encodes the image several times and keeps the highest quality that is still under your limit.

If even the lowest quality is too big — for example, a 12-megapixel photo squeezed into 20 KB — the image is also scaled down step by step until it fits. JPG is selected by default because almost every form accepts it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I compress a photo to exactly 50 KB?

Turn on Target file size (it's on by default here), tap 50 KB and add your photo. The result will be just under 50 KB, with the best quality possible at that size.

Why does my image get smaller in pixels too?

When a very small target can't be reached by lowering quality alone, the image is resized down in steps. This keeps it readable instead of turning it into blocky artifacts.

What is the difference between KB and MB?

1 MB is 1,024 KB. A typical phone photo is 2–5 MB (2,000–5,000 KB), so a 100 KB limit means shrinking it by about 95%.

Can I compress to a size in pixels as well?

Yes. Choose Exact size under Max dimension to set width and height in pixels, and keep the target file size on to control the KB at the same time.
