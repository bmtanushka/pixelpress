How to compress photos for email so they always send

Updated October 19, 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer: Resize photos to about 1920 px (or 1280 px for many photos) and compress at 80% quality. A typical phone photo shrinks from 3–5 MB to 200–500 KB, so you can attach dozens without hitting Gmail's or Outlook's attachment limits.

Email attachment limits

Most email services limit attachments to around 20–25 MB per message — Gmail and Yahoo Mail allow 25 MB and Outlook.com 20 MB. Attachments are also encoded for sending, which adds roughly a third to their size, so in practice you should stay well below the limit. Larger files are usually turned into cloud-storage links instead.

How big should an emailed photo be?

For viewing on screens, 1280–1920 pixels on the longest side is plenty, and 80% JPG quality looks the same as the original. That's typically 200–500 KB per photo — around a tenth of the original size.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my email say the attachment is too large?

The total size of all attachments, after encoding, is over the provider's limit. Compress or resize the photos, or send them in several emails.

Does compressing photos for email reduce quality?

At 1920 px and 80% quality the difference isn't visible on screens. Keep the originals if the photos will be printed.

Should I send photos as a ZIP?

A ZIP keeps them together and in order, but it doesn't make already-compressed photos much smaller. Compress them first.
